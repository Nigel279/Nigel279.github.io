Biotech Breakthroughs: Genetics Experiment

In this project, we explored how DNA works and what it can tell us about human traits and ancestry. We completed a lab that involved extracting DNA, copying it using PCR, and separating it using gel electrophoresis. The goal was to investigate whether race is a biological concept or a social construct based on genetic evidence.

Overview of the Lab

This lab was designed to help us understand how DNA can be extracted, copied, and analyzed. We worked with real scientific techniques used in genetics research today.

DNA Extraction

We began by extracting DNA from cheek cells. We used a small stick to gently collect cells from inside our cheeks. These cells contained the DNA needed for the experiment.

PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction)

Next, we used a PCR machine to make many copies of our DNA. The machine heated and cooled the samples repeatedly, allowing the DNA to be copied many times. After about 30 cycles, we had over one million copies of DNA.

Gel Electrophoresis

We then used gel electrophoresis to separate DNA fragments. Because DNA is negatively charged, it moves through a gel when electricity is applied. Smaller DNA fragments move farther than larger ones, allowing us to compare results. We also used a DNA ladder to measure fragment sizes.

Data and Predictions

After collecting our DNA results, we completed a survey to predict who we might match with. We used ethnicity and place of origin to make predictions before comparing results.

Conclusion

Our results showed that genetic similarity does not always match with race or appearance. This helped us understand that DNA is complex and that race is not a reliable biological classification.

What I Learned

This lab helped me understand how DNA is analyzed in real labs. I learned that genetics is complex and does not rely on physical features. I also learned that race is not a biological classification but a social concept.
